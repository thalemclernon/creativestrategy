# RYZE — Gut-Skin Axis Angle Playbook

## Section 1 — Copywriter Quick Reference

**The angle in one line:** The gut and skin are in constant communication — and a well-supported gut microbiome creates the internal environment that healthy skin depends on.

**What you CAN say:**

- "Supports gut health" (CL-0023, Low — primary headline claim)
- "Supports the growth of beneficial gut bacteria" (CL-0017, Low — microbiome framing)
- "Provides prebiotic fiber to nourish good gut bacteria" (CL-0017 alt, Low)
- "Supports immune function" (CL-0021, Low — gut-immune-skin chain)
- "Supports a healthy immune response" (CL-0021 alt, Low)
- "Supports digestive comfort" (CL-0001, Low — gut comfort framing)

**What you CANNOT say:**

- ❌ "Clears acne" / "reduces acne" / "treats breakouts" → disease claim
- ❌ "Treats eczema / psoriasis / rosacea" → disease claims
- ❌ "Reduces skin inflammation" → drug claim (NSAID territory)
- ❌ "Kills bad bacteria" → prohibited by CL-0017
- ❌ "Improves leaky gut" → disease claim framing
- ❌ "Repairs gut" → prohibited by CL-0017 and CL-0023
- ❌ "Restores gut microbiome" → prohibited by CL-0017
- ❌ "Boosts immune system" → prohibited by CL-0021

**The structural rule that makes this angle work:**

The gut-skin connection is the HOOK and EDUCATION (Box 1). The approved product claims are gut health and microbiome support — not skin. The skin angle brings the audience in; the gut claim closes the ad. Skin outcomes are never the product claim.

## Section 2 — Angle Overview

### No Approved Skin Claims Exist in the DB

RYZE currently has no approved skin health, skin clarity, or skin appearance claims. The gut-skin axis must be used as educational context only — never as a product claim.

**What this means in practice:**

- Hook: "What's happening in your gut can show up in your skin" ✅ (educational)
- Bridge: "Supporting your gut microbiome creates the internal environment healthy skin depends on" ✅ (bridge)
- Claim: "Supports gut health" / "Supports the growth of beneficial gut bacteria" ✅ (gut claim)
- NOT: "RYZE supports clearer skin" ❌ (no approved skin claim)

### The Breakout Sub-Angle — Highest Scrutiny

"Acne" is an FDA-recognized medical condition with defined OTC drug treatments. Any framing implying RYZE addresses acne — including gut-driven framing like "RYZE helps clear gut-related breakouts" — is a disease claim.

The breakout angle works only when: (1) the hook addresses the audience experience, (2) education explains the gut-skin connection mechanically, (3) the bridge moves to gut health, (4) the claim is a gut/microbiome claim — never skin.

**The distinction:** "Your gut microbiome influences the internal environment your skin lives in" (education ✅) vs. "RYZE supports clearer skin by improving your gut" (skin claim ❌).

### The Inflammation Sub-Angle

"Reduces skin inflammation" = drug claim (NSAID territory — same prohibition as the Joint Health playbook). The compliant approach uses the immune function or gut health claim as the mechanism; skin redness/reactivity is the hook context only.

**Applicable regulatory references:**

- REG-0006 (FDA Disease Claim Prohibition): Acne, eczema, psoriasis, rosacea = disease claims; leaky gut = disease framing
- REG-0001 (FTC Health Product Compliance): An ad structured as "fix your gut, clear your skin" creates a skin treatment net impression even when the literal claim is gut health
- REG-0003 (Creative Risk Matrix): Breakout and inflammation sub-angles require separation bridge

## Section 3 — Target Audience & Pain Points

### Primary Audience Segments

**Segment A — The Skincare Maximalist (Frustrated)**

*Age range:* 25–40, skews female. Has an extensive skincare routine — cleanser, serum, SPF, spot treatment, retinoid. Has probably seen a dermatologist. Does "everything right" topically and still has inconsistent results.

**Pain points:**

- "I've tried every product and my skin still breaks out"
- "My skin is fine for weeks then randomly reacts"
- "I've been on two different prescriptions and neither solved it long-term"
- "My dermatologist tells me my skin is 'normal' but it doesn't feel normal to me"

**The insight that creates relevance:** They've been optimizing the wrong system. The topical approach is correct but incomplete — the gut-skin axis reframes their problem as having an interior component they've never addressed. Hook framing: acknowledge their investment, don't dismiss it, then introduce the inside gap.

**Best sub-angles:** Skin Clarity / Complexion; Breakouts (cautious framing — see Section 5)

---

**Segment B — The Gut-Health Aware**

*Age range:* 28–45. Already has a gut health framework — has tried probiotics, tracks digestive comfort, uses terms like "microbiome" and "gut lining." May not have connected gut health to skin outcomes explicitly yet.

**Pain points:**

- "I've been supporting my gut but my skin is still inconsistent"
- "I know gut health matters but I haven't made the skin connection clearly"
- "I've taken probiotics for years — is there something more targeted for the gut-skin link?"

**The insight that creates relevance:** They don't need to be sold on gut health — they need the gut-to-skin pathway explained and RYZE positioned within it. The mushroom beta-glucan / prebiotic angle is highly credible to this audience; no need to oversell.

**Best sub-angles:** Skin Clarity; Educational mechanism content (this audience tolerates and rewards more science)

---

**Segment C — The Reactive Skin Type**

*Age range:* 30–50. Deals with chronic skin sensitivity, redness, or texture issues that aren't classifiable as acne or a named condition. Has tried elimination diets, calming skincare, and dermatology with partial results.

**Pain points:**

- "My skin reacts to things I can't predict"
- "Redness that appears for no obvious reason"
- "My skin is fine for a while, then something flares it — I can never pin down what"

**The insight that creates relevance:** Reactive skin can have a systemic driver. The gut immune function → inflammatory signaling pathway explains variability that seems random from a topical perspective.

**Best sub-angles:** Skin Inflammation / Redness; Immune function framing (CL-0021)

## Section 8 — Core Mechanism, Ingredient Story & Evidence

### The Gut-Skin Axis — Core Mechanism

About 70% of the body's immune activity is regulated in the gut. The gut microbiome — hundreds of trillions of bacteria, fungi, and other microorganisms — influences inflammatory signaling throughout the body. The gut lining and the skin are both **barrier tissues**: they separate the external world from the internal environment, and they share key immune regulation pathways.

**When microbiome balance is well-supported:**

- The gut lining's barrier function remains intact
- Immune signaling (systemic cytokine profiles) is more precise and less inflammatory in tone
- Microbiome-derived metabolites — including short-chain fatty acids — circulate systemically and influence skin barrier function

**When microbiome balance is disrupted:**

- Gut-immune signaling becomes less regulated
- Systemic inflammatory tone may rise
- Skin — one of the first organs to reflect internal immune states — responds accordingly

This bidirectional communication between gut and skin, mediated by immune signaling and shared barrier biology, is called the **gut-skin axis**. Supporting the gut microbiome supports the environment your skin lives in.

### RYZE Ingredient Story

**Beta-glucan (from all 6 mushrooms — primary mechanism)**

Beta-glucan is a soluble prebiotic fiber found in all RYZE mushrooms. It functions as food for beneficial gut bacteria (Lactobacillus, Bifidobacterium), directly supporting microbiome diversity and growth — the mechanism behind CL-0017. Beta-glucan also binds to Dectin-1 receptors on innate immune cells, activating immune modulation — the mechanism behind CL-0021.

**Lion's Mane**

Highest beta-glucan concentration in the RYZE blend. Primary prebiotic fiber contributor. Additionally contains hericenones and erinacines studied for neurological signaling support.

**Reishi**

Contains ganoderic acids (triterpenes) alongside beta-glucan. Published evidence for gut microbiome diversity modulation and systemic immune regulation. Most extensively studied mushroom for immune modulation.

**King Trumpet**

Rich in beta-glucan and ergothioneine — a rare antioxidant amino acid with evidence for protecting gut lining integrity and reducing oxidative stress. Supports immune function and gut microbiome health; prebiotic activity supports Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium growth alongside Lion's Mane.

**Shiitake**

Contains lentinan — a well-studied beta-glucan polysaccharide with strong immune-modulating evidence, including published clinical research on NK cell activation. Rich in B vitamins that support gut lining health and cellular energy. Lentinan is one of the most researched single immune compounds in the functional mushroom category.

**Turkey Tail**

Contains PSK (polysaccharide-K) and PSP — two of the most-studied immune-modulating mushroom compounds in the literature. Highest individual evidence volume for CL-0021 (immune function) in the RYZE blend.

**Cordyceps**

Primary mechanism: mitochondrial energy support (ATP synthesis). Secondary: beta-glucan contribution. Supports gut-energy-immune cascade when fatigue framing layers into the angle.

## Section 9 — Creative Formats & Placement

### Approved Visual Approaches

- Close-up skin texture — healthy, glowing, natural (not clinical, not "before")
- Gut-as-garden imagery — abstract, warm, botanical. Avoids clinical organ visualization.
- Morning routine layering: RYZE product alongside (not replacing) skincare products
- Product in a wellness morning ritual context
- Microbiome-as-ecosystem metaphor — soil, flora, garden imagery (not anatomical)

### Prohibited Visual Territory

- Before/after skin transformations (implies treatment outcome)
- Acne, inflamed skin, or "problem skin" close-ups (disease imagery)
- Anatomical gut or intestine diagrams (see Organ Imagery Risk Multiplier below)
- Clinical/medical setting imagery
- Any imagery framing RYZE as a skin treatment

### Organ Imagery Risk Multiplier

**If copy references gut microbiome, gut lining, or digestive system AND the visual shows anatomical gut or intestinal imagery, risk escalates one full tier** (Low → Moderate; Moderate → Moderate-High). Keep gut-related visuals in the abstract / botanical / lifestyle zone — not anatomical. The garden metaphor and microbiome-as-ecosystem framing are intentional: they allow the gut-skin concept to be visualized without triggering organ imagery risk.

**Safe visual zone:** ecosystem imagery, botanical motifs, morning ritual lifestyle, healthy glowing skin

**Risk zone:** anatomical cross-sections, gut diagrams, intestinal anatomy, clinical organ imagery

### Screenshot Test Guidance

1. **Visual alone:** Healthy skin = compliant. Inflamed/problem skin = disease claim visual.
2. **Headline alone:** "Supports gut health" reads as compliant. "Supports clearer skin" = unapproved claim.
3. **Visual + Headline:** Skin visual + gut health claim = compliant. Skin visual + skin outcome claim = unapproved.
4. **Targeting context:** Acne-specific audience targeting + any breakout framing = elevated net impression risk — reassess before launch.
